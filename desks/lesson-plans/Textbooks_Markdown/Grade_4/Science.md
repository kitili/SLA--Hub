## Health principles

### Introduction
Good health is important for our well-being. In this chapter, you will learn about health principles. These include eating a balanced diet, doing physical exercises and maintaining both personal and environmental cleanliness. However, you will also explore safety signs and other important health information in the environment. The competencies developed will enable you to follow health principles. You will also be able to protect your health and the health of others.

## Concept of health principles
Following health principles is important for staying healthy. Good health means being strong in both body and mind. To be healthy, you need to eat a balanced diet, do physical exercises regularly and ensure personal and environmental cleanliness. It is also important to follow safety signs and health information in the environment. Figure 1 shows examples of health principles.

### Balanced diet

A balanced diet is a meal with all the food nutrients in the correct proportions. It is made up of foods from three main groups. The groups are energy-giving foods, body-building foods, protective foods. Energy-giving foods have carbohydrate and fat nutrients. Body-building foods have protein nutrients. Protective foods have vitamins and minerals nutrients. Eating a balanced diet helps to make the body's immune system strong to fight off diseases. **Benefits of exercising**

It is good to do physical exercises regularly. Regular physical exercises help to:

(a) Stay healthy and strong;
(b) Keep body systems like digestion and excretory systems working well;
(c) Strengthen body immunity to fight different diseases;
(d) Reduce stress and depression; and

(a) Having poor health and weak body;
(b) Gaining too much weight, leading to obesity;

(d) Feeling stressed and depressed; and
(e) Having weak muscles and bones.

### Personal hygiene

Personal hygiene is important for our health. The body and clothes should be cleaned using proper tools to avoid diseases. Personal hygiene includes cleaning the mouth, skin, hair, nails and clothes.

#### Oral hygiene

The mouth should be cleaned using a toothbrush, toothpaste, and clean, safe water. Brushing removes food particles from the teeth. If the food particles remain, bacteria in the mouth feed on them and produce acid. This acid damages the teeth and causes cavities. It is important to brush your teeth at least twice a day. Brush your teeth in the morning when you wake up and at night before going to bed. This helps keep your teeth healthy.

#### Bathing

Clean water and soap should be used for bathing. It is good to bathe every day, in the morning and evening to avoid skin diseases. If we do not bathe, our bodies can smell bad. After bathing, dry the body with a clean towel. Apply body oil to keep the skin soft and prevent it from drying. We should also wash our faces with clean water and soap after waking up. This helps remove rheum and prevent eye diseases.

### *Hair hygiene*

Hair should be cared for by regularly washing with clean water and soap. It is also important to care the hair by braiding, shaving or combing to look clean and neat. Keeping hair clean helps prevent dandruff and pests like lice. This also helps to prevent scalp infections such as ringworms.

### *Nail hygiene*

Fingernails and toenails should be kept short and clean by cutting them regularly. Long nails can hold dirt with germs that cause diseases. Washing hands and nails with clean running water and soap helps remove and kill these germs.

### *Clothing hygiene*

Clothes should be washed with clean water and soap to remove dirt. Wearing dirty clothes can cause body odour and lead to skin problems like itching and rashes. After washing, clothes should be properly dried and ironed. Ironing helps kill germs and keeps the clothes looking neat.

## Diseases

### Introduction

Diseases make people unhealthy. In this chapter, you will learn about human diseases. You will also learn how diseases spread, their causes and how to prevent them. You will also learn the life cycle of some insects that spread various diseases. The competencies developed will enable you to protect yourself and others from diseases.

[diagram: Icon of a person thinking] **Think**: The effects of diseases on human health

### Concept of diseases

Disease is an abnormal condition or disorders in a living organism that disrupts normal bodily function. Many diseases are caused by germs found in our surroundings. These germs are found in air, soil, water, unsafe food and animal bodily fluids like blood. Germs can enter the body through eating contaminated food, drinking unsafe water, breathing in or touching an infected person. However, some diseases are not caused by germs, such as hereditary diseases.

### Communicable diseases

Communicable diseases are diseases that spread from one person to another. These diseases are caused by pathogens like bacteria and viruses. Examples of communicable diseases are malaria, cholera, chickenpox, tuberculosis, hepatitis and Corona Virus Disease (COVID-19).

#### *Malaria*

Malaria is a disease caused by parasites called plasmodium. The disease is spread by a female mosquito called Anopheles.

## Symptoms of malaria

Symptoms of malaria include the following:

(a) Having a high fever due to the raising of body temperature;
(b) Feeling cold and shivering;
(c) Headache;
(d) General body weakness;
(e) Vomiting; and
(f) Loss of appetite.

### Control and prevention of malaria

Malaria can be controlled by destroying places where mosquitoes breed. These places include bushes, long grass, and stagnant water in puddles. Tyres, coconut shells and cans that hold water can also serve as breeding areas for mosquitoes. Kerosene or larvicide can be spread on puddles to kill mosquito larvae. Also, coconut shells and other breeding areas should be destroyed. Malaria can also be prevented by sleeping under a treated mosquito net. Other ways include using mosquito sprays, coils and repellents. Also, to put nets on the windows of the house to prevent mosquitoes from entering. If someone has malaria symptoms, they should go to the hospital for diagnosis and treatment.

## Program control structures

Program control structures refer to how the flow of computer instructions is managed. There are three types of program control structures namely sequence, repetition and selection.

### Sequence structure in programming

In programming, a sequence structure follows the order of flow of instructions or blocks from top to bottom. The first block is executed before the next block. For example, in Figure 32, blocks will be executed as follows.

1. The '*when clicked*' block is executed first.
2. The '*go to (random position)*' block is executed next.
3. The '*turn (↶) 15 degrees*' block is then executed.
4. Lastly, the '*if on edge, bounce*' block is executed.

[diagram: Visual representation of a Scratch programming sequence. From top to bottom, the blocks are: 1. "when clicked" (with green flag), 2. "go to random position", 3. "turn ↶ 15 degrees", 4. "if on edge, bounce". A red arrow on the left points downwards to indicate the sequential flow of execution.]

**Figure 32**: *Sequence structure in programming*

Thus, the flow becomes as follows: '*When clicked → go to (random position) → turn (15) degrees → if on edge, bounce*'.

In computer programming, you might need some action to repeat several times. For example, you might need movement
