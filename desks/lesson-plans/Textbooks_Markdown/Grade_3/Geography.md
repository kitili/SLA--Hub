## The concepts of Geography and the Environment

### Introduction

Human beings need to understand the relationship between Geography and the Environment so that they know the best ways to interact with their surroundings. Geography enables human beings to understand the surrounding environment and to conserve and use it sustainably. In this chapter, you will learn about the meanings, interaction and importance of learning Geography and the Environment. The competencies to be developed will enable you to value and care for the environment.

Geography is a subject that deals with the study of human beings and their environment. Geography is concerned with the earth, the atmosphere and things that exist on the earth’s surface. ### Importance of learning Geography and the Environment

Learning Geography and the Environment enables us to understand our surroundings. It also helps us to recognise the resources we have in our environment and how to use, conserve and manage them. Examples of such resources are wildlife, rivers, lakes, oceans, forests, land, minerals and mountains.

Similarly, learning Geography and the Environment enables us to understand the human activities that can be undertaken in different areas. For example, in areas with rivers, lakes and oceans, fishing or tourism can be practised. Plain land is mostly used for agriculture and livestock keeping. Lumbering and beekeeping are undertaken in areas with forests.

Furthermore, learning Geography and the Environment enables us to understand the local weather condition of a particular area. For example, an area dominated by trees often has pleasant weather and is attractive. In addition, mountainous areas may have cooler weather due to the high altitude; thus people need to wear thick

--- PAGE 17 ---

<!--folio:10-->
clothes like sweaters when they are in such areas. Coastal areas often have warmer weather than inland areas. Therefore, those in warm coastal areas can wear lighter clothing to avoid or reduce the effects of heat. However, the temperature in coastal areas can vary depending on the elevation of land and the direction of winds. Geography and the Environment helps us to identify natural hazards such as floods, earthquakes and hurricanes, and how to avoid their effects on people and their property. Geography and the Environment also assists us in planning and building infrastructure and social services. Examples of infrastructure and providing social services include roads, hospitals and schools. Thus, learning Geography and the Environment enables human beings to identify areas in need of such services. Generally, it is important to learn Geography and the Environment so as to plan for appropriate and sustainable use of the resources found in the environment. The resources can be water, forests and land.

## The earth's relief

### Introduction
The earth surface is covered by a hard layer known as land. Land has various shapes that form earth’s relief. In this chapter, you will learn about the meaning of the earth’s relief, the major relief features, and the distribution and importance of such features. The competencies to be developed will enable you to interact well with the relief features in your environment.

### Think
[diagram: drawing of a person thinking]
Various features of the earth's relief you have seen

### Meaning of the earth's relief
The earth's relief is the appearance of various shapes of land on the earth's surface. These shapes create different kind of landscape on the earth's surface. The earth's relief consists of a distribution of relief features such as mountains, hills, plains, plateaus, basins and valleys. Human activities such as farming, construction and livestock keeping depend on the relief features of a given place. Therefore, it is important for us to understand the relief features so that we can interact with them in a sustainable manner.

### Mountains

A mountain is a landform with the highest altitude above the earth's surface. The top part of a mountain is called a mountain peak or summit. The peak of a mountain may have snow and ice on it or not. In addition, a mountain has steep slopes. Tanzania has several large and small mountains. For example, the highest mountain in Tanzania is Mount Kilimanjaro, which is located in Kilimanjaro Region. Mount Kilimanjaro has a height of 5,895 meters. The top of Mount Kilimanjaro is covered with ice and snow. This is beacuse, as you go higher, temperature decreases. Mount Kilimanjaro is also the highest mountain in Africa. Hills are found in various places in Tanzania. Some of the hills in Tanzania are Mererani in Manyara and Ngorongoro in Arusha, Sao and Kitonga in Iringa, and the Sekenke hills in Singida Region. ## Valleys

Valleys are areas of lower land which are found between two hills or mountains, as shown in Figure 6.

[diagram: A river flowing through a steep, green valley]

**Figure 6**: A valley

Some valleys have rivers flowing through them. Lakes may also form within valleys. For example, Tanzania has a major East African rift valley with several lakes. The lakes in this valley include Lake Natron in Arusha Region and Eyasi and Manyara in Manyara region. Others lakes in the east African rift valley are Lake Tanganyika in Kigoma, Katavi and Rukwa Regions. Lake Tanganyika is the deepest lake in Tanzania and Africa. There are also Lake Nyasa in Ruvuma, Njombe and Mbeya Regions.

<!--folio:23-->

### The importance of valleys

Valleys are important because they collect water from elevated areas. Thus, valleys are an important source of water. Areas with valleys that contain lakes and rivers are habitats for aquatic organisms such as fish. Therefore, fishing is undertaken in these areas. Some valleys have fertile soil; therefore, they are suitable for irrigation farming. Additionally, the rivers in valleys can be dammed to generate hydro-electric power. Lakes and some rivers are useful for transporting people and goods using vessels such as ships and boats.

### Plateaus

A plateau is a large area of flat elevated land that is relatively raised sharply above the surrounding land. Figure 7 shows the view of a plateau.

Plateaus vary in shape and size. An example of a plateau in Tanzania is the Southern highland plateau in the regions of Iringa, Mbeya and Njombe.

### The importance of plateaus
Some plateaus are used for human settlements and preserved as tourist attractions due to their beautiful outlook and pleasant weather. For example, Kitulo is a national park situated in the Southern highland plateau in Mbeya and Njombe. This park is located between the peaks of Kipengele, Uporoto and Livingstone mountain ranges. This is the first reserve in Africa to be established so as to conserve its natural plants and flowers. Other parts of the plateau are used for agricultural purposes.

## Environmental conservation

### Introduction

The well-being of humans and other organisms depends on essential resources such as land, air and water which are found in their environment. Sustainable use of these resources depends on environmental conservation. Therefore, it is important for the society to understand environmental conservation so as to ensure sustainable utilization of available resources. In this chapter, you will learn about the meaning and importance of conserving the environment, and learn various ways of conserving the environment. The competencies to be developed will enable you to conserve the environment so as to achieve sustainable development.

# Meaning of environmental conservation

### Activity 1
Observe your school environment, and then explain:
1. the cleanliness of the school environment; and
2. the advantages or disadvantages of keeping the environment in the condition you have seen.

Environmental conservation is the act of protecting and preserving the things that surround human beings. The things includes forests, land, rivers, valleys, animals, mountains, air and oceans. Environmental conservation activities in Tanzania are carried out at different levels. The government has established institutions and offices to conserve the environment. There are environmental conservation offices at village, street and ward levels. There are also environmental conservation offices at district, regional and national levels. The offices at different levels are managed by environmental officers. The officers collaborate with government leaders to manage the environment.

Moreover, at each level there are environmental management committees. The committees include that of health and the environment. These committees are responsible to mobilize communities to participate in environmental management activities. These activities include planting and caring for trees. It is the responsibility of the government at each level to make by-laws and impose fines on anyone caught destroying the environment. In addition, the government of Tanzania

--- PAGE 38 ---

<!--folio:31-->

established the National Environmental Management Council (NEMC) in 1983 so that it could advise the government on matters pertaining to environmental management. Thus, we are supposed to conserve our environment to protect ourselves and other organisms.

## The importance of environmental conservation

### Activity 2

Use library or Internet sources to explore the importance of environmental conservation.

An environment that is clean and safe is pleasant, attractive and crucial for our well-being. A dirty environment hides insects and other harmful organisms. The insects and organisms include fleas, flies, mosquitoes, rats and snakes. The insects and organisms usually hide in a dirty environment.

Clean and safe environments prevent the spread of diseases such as skin diseases, diarrhoea, malaria, the bubonic plague and other kinds of diseases associated with a dirty environment. ## Livestock keeping

Livestock keeping is an activity that involves rearing of livestock such as cows, goats, sheep, chickens and ducks. There are three types of livestock keeping: zero-grazing, free-range rearing and nomadic pastoralism. In zero-grazing, animals are kept and fed indoors. These large farms are called ranches.

Another type of livestock keeping is free-range rearing. In this kind of animal rearing, pastoralists take their livestock to grazing areas in the morning and bring them back in the evening. The other type of livestock keeping is called nomadic pastoralism. In this kind of livestock keeping, pastoralists move with their livestock from place to place, looking for pastures and water. Sometimes, livestock travel long distances before they reach a place where pastures and water are available.

## Fishing

Salt water sources include oceans, while fresh water sources are rivers, lakes, ponds and dams. Some of the salt-water fish are snapper, rabbit fish, octopuses, mackerel, jacks, kingfish and sharks. Examples of freshwater fish are tilapia, catfish and the Nile perch. Tanzania is rich in fishing areas, including the Indian Ocean, Lake Victoria, Lake Tanganyika and Lake Nyasa. ## Effects of fishing on the environment
Fishermen use different ways to catch fish. Fishing methods can be appropriate or inappropriate to the environment. Appropriate fishing methods include the use of proper nets that are standard and ideal for fishing. Inappropriate fishing methods are those that damage fish and destroy their breeding grounds. These methods involve using poisons and explosives. Poisons and explosives destroy marine and fresh-water environments. Poisons and explosives kill fish and other aquatic organisms. The breeding grounds for aquatic organisms are also destroyed by poisons and expolosives, leading to the extinction of marine life. Poisoned fish, in turn, affect consumers by causing diseases such as cancer. Moreover, fishing using seine nets is one of the improper fishing ways because it catches everything, including small fish, eggs and other aquatic organisms. Environmental degradation contributes to climate change in an area. The average weather condition in a specific area recorded for 30 years or more is known as climate. Climate includes various elements of weather such as temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind and atmospheric pressure.

The climate of an area changes with a change in its weather elements over a long period of time. Climate change is caused by natural processes such as volcanic eruptions and human activities that produce carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases that cause an increase in temperature. Climate change is largely associated with human activities that produce a large quantity of carbon dioxide. When carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases are released into the atmosphere, they create a layer that prevents heat from escaping from the earth's surface. This process causes an increase in temperature on the earth's surface.

Rising temperature leads to various challenges. For example, in Tanzania, since 2012, the increase in temperature has caused a significant reduction in the amount of ice and snow on the peak of Mount Kilimanjaro. The ice and snow on the world's highest mountains and in the polar regions have also melted due to the increase in temperature resulting from climate change. When ice melts, it turns into water that flows into the sea thus, causing the rise in sea level. The rise in sea level causes floods in coastal areas.

Likewise, climate change leads to either droughts or heavy rainfall that may result in floods and strong winds.

Climate change is also associated with the increase in , and the outbreak of diseases that did not previously exist. For example, highland areas that did not experience malaria before, now have malaria-causing mosquitoes due to the increase in temperature as a result of climate change. All these have significant effects on human beings and other organisms.

## Climate change mitigation measures

Climate change can be mitigated by forest conservation and afforestation. Forests and trees absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. Carbon dioxide contributes

--- PAGE 83 ---

<!--folio:76-->

to global warming. The use of alternative energy sources such as solar, natural gas, hydroelectricity and wind help to reduce excessive cutting of trees. The air pollution caused by greenhouse gases from industries, the aviation industry and vehicles which contribute to global warming should be controlled.

In farming, people have to use appropriate industrial fertilisers, especially those rich in nitrogen. This is because the nitrous oxide gas emitted during agricultural activities contributes to global warming when it is released into the atmosphere. Therefore, farmers are advised to practise organic farming, which is sustainable and environment-friendly.

## Adaptation to the effects of climate change

Climate change often affects the environment surrounding us. The effects of climate change such as drought, extreme heat and floods pose serious challenges to humans such as famine and diseases. Since climate change will continue to exist, it is necessary to adapt to both its current and future effects. Some of the strategies for adapting to the effects of climate change are planting drought-tolerant and disease-resistant crops; and crops that mature early such as millet, sweet potatoes, cassava, pulses and sorghum. In areas where irrigation is practised, it is important to use the drip irrigation system that uses a little amount of water. Furthermore, the harvesting and storage of rain water for use during water shortages is another way of adapting to the effects of climate change.
