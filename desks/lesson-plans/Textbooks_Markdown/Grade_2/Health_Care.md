# Introduction

This book, Health and Environment Standard Two, has been prepared based on the curriculum and syllabus for Primary Education Standards I and II for the year 2023, issued by the Ministry of Education, Science, and Technology. Moreover, the book is the result of improvements and the combination of the two Health and Environment books for Standards One and Two, published in 2019 based on the 2016 curriculum and syllabus. The objective of this book is to enable pupils to build competency in taking care of health and the environment through the following learning activities:

1. Simple investigation of living and non-living things.
2. Adhering to safety signs.
3. Taking care of the environment.

Additional learning resources are available at TIE e-Library https://ol.tie.go.tz/videos

**Read the following case study and answer the questions that follow:**

Sopwe and Kipepe are standard two pupils at Mtakuja Primary School. The school is located along a main road that has a children’s crossing sign. One day, Sopwe and Kipepe went to buy sweets from a shop located across the road. They crossed a section of the road that did not have a children’s crossing sign. As they crossed, they were struck by a motorcycle. Sopwe broke his arm, and Kipepe sustained bruises on his knees. Their classmates called a teacher, who took them to the hospital for treatment. The teacher instructed them always use the children’s crossing whenever they cross the road. Additionally, they should ensure that bicycles, motorcycles, and cars have stopped before crossing.

**Read the following case study, then answer the questions.**

Chausiku is a Standard Two pupil at Upepo Primary School. One day, she had a high fever and was taken

--- PAGE 35 ---

<!--folio:29-->

to a health center for treatment. After recovering, she returned to school. Chausiku told her classmates that she had malaria. Her classmates wanted to know the causes of the disease. Chausiku explained, as the doctor had told her, that "Malaria is caused by parasites that are transmitted to human through mosquitoes. Mosquitoes breed in environments with stagnant water, long grass, and in containers and tires that hold water. To prevent malaria, it is necessary to remove mosquito breeding sites by cutting long grasses and removing stagnant water. Additionally, containers and tires that hold water should be removed from our environment. Moreover, cleaning the environment keeps away other dangerous animals like snakes, monitor lizards, scorpions, and centipedes. The pupils were happy and promised to clean their environment.
