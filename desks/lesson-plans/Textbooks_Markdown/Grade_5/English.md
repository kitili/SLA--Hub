# Unit One

## Developing vocabulary

### Introduction
Vocabulary is one of the most important aspects of learning a language for everyday communication. One cannot communicate effectively if he or she does not know the vocabulary of the target language. In this unit, you will learn words that mean the opposite. You will also learn words that mean the same, and solve word puzzles from various media. Finally, you will write short passages using vocabulary referring to objects in natural settings. The competences developed will enable you to communicate meanings clearly.

***

[diagram: icon of a person thinking] Think about how we could communicate in English without adequate vocabulary.

***

### Learning words that mean the opposite

*Read the passage and look at the pictures of elephants to answer the questions that follow.*

Words are powerful tools that help us express what we see, feel and think. One way to develop our vocabulary is by learning words that mean the opposite of each other. These are called **opposites** or **antonyms**. For example, the antonym of "hot" is "cold" and the antonym of "happy" is "sad". Knowing the antonyms helps us speak and write more clearly and understand what others say more easily.

*Read the passage and look at the pictures of buildings to answer the questions that follow.*

Did you know that many words in English can mean the same thing? These are called **synonyms**. For example, "big" and "large" are synonyms because they both describe something of great size. ## Comparing things

### Introduction

In our daily lives, we often compare people, animals, objects, or places based on their size, quality, or quantity to understand them better. In this unit, you will learn to compare things using the right words to show similarities or differences in size, quality, and quantity. You will learn to compare things using words like big, bigger, and biggest, or small, smaller, and smallest among other words. You will also learn to use words such as ‘much,’ ‘more,’ and ‘most’ to show comparisons. The competences developed will enable you to speak English better.

### Did you know?

The pictures of stones we have seen differ in size. **Stone A** is big, but **Stone B** is bigger than **Stone A**. **Stone C** is bigger than **Stone B** and **Stone A**. Thus, **Stone C** is the biggest because it is bigger than **Stone A and B**.

We often talk about how things are different or similar. We compare things to describe them better, for example, we might say one box is bigger than the other, or one toy is bigger than the other. When we compare things, we use words that show how something looks as compared to another similar thing or how someone looks compared to someone else. We may use words to show differences in size, such as big, bigger, biggest, or large, larger, largest, or small, smaller, smallest. We may also use words to show differences in quality, such as as good, better, best, or clean, cleaner, cleanest.

***

### Did you also know?
We use comparatives and superlatives to compare people, animals, or things. Comparatives show differences between two things. For example, "A ruler is longer than a pencil".

Superlatives show characteristics of something or someone that stands above the rest: the highest or lowest quality among three or more things, people, or other objects. For example, "Lake Tanganyika is the deepest lake in Africa," or "Lake Victoria is the largest freshwater lake in Africa".
