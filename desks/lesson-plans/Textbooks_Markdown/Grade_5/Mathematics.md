# Introduction

This textbook is prepared for a Standard Five Pupil in Tanzania Mainland. Mathematics Pupil’s Book for Standard Five is written per the 2023 Mathematics Syllabus for Primary School Education Standard III – VI issued by the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology. This is the second edition after the improvement of the Standard Five Textbook published in 2019 as based on the 2016 Mathematics Syllabus for Primary School Education Standard III – VII.

This book consists of seven chapters: Measurements of length, mass and volume; Circumference and area of a circle; Types, factors and multiples of numbers; Fractions; Decimals; Algebra; and Financial mathematics. The competencies developed will enable you to acquire basic mathematical skills and their applications, logical thinking and solving real-life problems.

Content of the chapters are presented interms of texts, drawings, pictures, activities and exercises. You are advised to engage yourself and with friends in all activities and exercises in this book and other works provided with your teacher to develop the intended competencies.

Additional learning resources are available in the TIE-e-Library at *https://ol.tie.go.tz* or *ol.tie.go.tz*

## Division of metric units

The steps for dividing metric units are not different from those of multiplication. The important thing to remember is the relationship between the metric units. The only difference is that when dividing metric units, you start from the largest to the smallest unit.

### Example 1

A piece of fabric whose length is 30 m and 60 cm was divided into 6 equal pieces. What was the length of each piece?

| Steps | Solution |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1. Arrange units of length in columns, then insert the division symbol | <pre>   m    cm<br>  ________<br>6) 30   60</pre> |
| 2. Divide by considering the relationship of the units of length. For example, 1 m = 100 cm<br><br>3. Divide 30 m by 6, to get 5 m. Write 5 in the m column<br><br>4. Divide 60 cm by 6 to get 10 cm<br><br>5. Write 10 in the cm column | <pre>   m    cm<br>   5    10<br>  ________<br>6) 30   60</pre> |

Therefore, the answer is 5 m 10 cm.

### Example 3

5000 L and 240 mL of oil are to be stored in 8 barrels of the same capacity. How many litres of oil will each barrel hold?

**Solution**

To know the capacity of each barrel, divide the given volume by the number of barrels.

Arrange units of volume in columns, then follow the steps of dividing the units of volume.

$$\begin{array}{rcc}
& \text{L} & \text{mL} \\
8\big) & 5000 & 240
\end{array}
=
\begin{array}{cc}
\text{L} & \text{mL} \\
625 & 30
\end{array}$$

Therefore, each barrel will hold 625 L and 30 mL of oil.

## Circumference of a circle

The circumference of a circle is the distance around the circle. This is the length of a string that goes around a circle from the start to the end.

For example, consider the bicycle tyre ABCDA whose centre is at point O. Circumference of the bicycle tyre is the distance sorounding bicycle tyre. This is the length of rope sorounding the bicycle tyre from point A, B, C, D turning to point A.

[diagram: A bicycle tyre with center point O, showing dashed line diameters AC and BD, with points A, B, C, and D marked around the outer edge.]

$\overline{\text{AC}}$ is the diameter of the circle of the bicycle tyre and the distance from the centre of the circle of the bicycle tyre to any point on the circumference of a circle of the bicycle tyre is called the radius. Thus, $\overline{\text{OB}}$, $\overline{\text{OA}}$ and $\overline{\text{OC}}$ are the radii of the circle of the bicycle tyre.

The diameter of a circle is denoted by a letter $d$, and its radius is denoted by a letter $r$. Hence, $d = 2r$.

Given the diameter or radius of the circular object, the circumference of a circular object is therefore given by $C = \pi d$ or $C = 2\pi r$ where $C$ is a circumference of the circular object.

Therefore, length of the rectangular shape = $\frac{C}{2} = \frac{2\pi r}{2} = \pi r$

Also, the width of the rectangle is equal to the radius of the circle.

Therefore, the area of a circle can be obtained by calculating the area of the rectangle constructed. This is possible because the length and width of the rectangle constructed are known. Therefore, you can write the formulae for the area of a circle as follows:

The area of a circle = Area of rectangular shape
$$= \text{length} \times \text{width}$$
$$= \pi r \times r$$
$$= \pi r^2$$

Therefore, the area of a circle is $\pi r^2$ where $r$ is the radius of the circle.

Now, use the formula with $\pi = 3.14$ to calculate the area of the circle, then, compare the answer with the area of the rectangular shape you have constructed. The answers are expected to be approximately the same.

Since, diameter ($d$) = 2 $\times$ radius ($r$)
$$d = 2r$$
$$r = \frac{d}{2}$$

Thus, area of a circle $= \pi r^2 = \frac{\pi d^2}{4}$

Therefore, the area of a circle is given by $\frac{\pi d^2}{4}$ or $\pi r^2$ where $r$ is the radius and $d$ is the diameter.

$$= 616\text{ mm}^2$$

$$= 88 \times 88 \times \frac{7}{88}$$

$$= 616\text{ m}$$

## Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

The Greatest Common Factor (GCF) is the largest number that divides two or more numbers without a remainder.

### Example 1

Find the common factors of 6 and 8.

**Solution**

List the factors of 6 and 8:
The factors of 6 are 1, 2, 3 and 6.
The factors of 8 are 1, 2, 4 and 8.
Therefore, the common factors of 6 and 8 are 1 and 2.

### Example 2

Use the listing method to find the GCF of 30 and 42.

**Solution**

List the factors of 30 and 42.
The factors of 30 are 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, and 30.
The factors of 42 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 14, 21, and 42.
The common factors of 30 and 42 are 1, 2, 3 and 6.
The greatest common factor of 30 and 42 is 6.
Therefore, the GCF of 30 and 42 is 6.

### Example 3

Find the GCF of 12 and 18 by using prime factors.

**Solution**

Express 12 as a product of prime factors: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3.

Express 18 as a product of prime factors: 18 = 2 × 3 × 3.

The common prime factors of both numbers are 2 and 3.

The product of 2 and 3 is 6.

The product of common prime factors is the GCF.

Therefore, the GCF of 12 and 18 is 6.

### Types of fractions

There are three main types of fractions which are proper fractions, improper fractions, and mixed numbers

### (a) Proper fractions

In a proper fraction, the numerator is less than the denominator. It means, the value of a proper fraction is always less than 1. The following are examples of proper fractions:

$$\frac{1}{4}, \frac{2}{4}, \frac{1}{9}, \frac{3}{8}, \frac{10}{12}, \frac{15}{40}$$

### (b) Improper fractions

An improper fraction is the opposite of a proper fraction. In an improper fraction, the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator. The value of an improper fraction is always 1 or

more. The following are examples of an improper fractions:

$$\frac{6}{5}, \frac{9}{4}, \frac{10}{7}, \frac{11}{9}, \frac{12}{11}, \frac{4}{3}.$$

### (c) Mixed numbers

A mixed number is a combination of a whole number and a proper fraction. It is obtained when an improper fraction is simplified. The following are examples of mixed numbers:

$$3\frac{1}{2}, 4\frac{1}{2}, 5\frac{3}{4}, 15\frac{1}{4}, 22\frac{1}{3}, 10\frac{1}{3}.$$

## Addition of mixed numbers

Addition of mixed numbers is done by changing mixed number into an improper fraction, then add the improper fraction and finally change back to a mixed number.

### Example 1

$$2\frac{1}{4} + 1\frac{3}{5} =$$

**Solution**

Take the denominator, multiply by the whole number, then add the numerator:

$$2\frac{1}{4} + 1\frac{3}{5} = \frac{9}{4} + \frac{8}{5}$$

Find the LCM of 4 and 5 which is 20

Convert fractions with different denominators to a common denominator

$$\frac{9}{4} = \frac{9 \times 5}{4 \times 5} = \frac{45}{20}$$

$$\frac{8}{5} = \frac{8 \times 4}{5 \times 4} = \frac{32}{20}$$

Add fractions with the same denominator

$$\frac{45}{20} + \frac{32}{20} = \frac{45 + 32}{20}$$

$$= \frac{77}{20} \text{ or } 3\frac{17}{20}$$

Therefore, $$2\frac{1}{4} + 1\frac{3}{5} = \frac{77}{20} \text{ or } 3\frac{17}{20}.$$

Algebra is used in everyday life to simplify mathematical calculations. For example; 4 families rear chicken and each family has the same number of chicken. ## Addition and subtraction of algebraic terms

Terms in the algebraic expression are said to be alike if they have the same variable, such as $4k$, $8k$, $5k$ and $7k$ . Like terms can be added or substracted to obtain a simpler algebraic term. In adding like terms, you add the coefficients and leave the variable unchanged. Terms which have different variables are called unlike terms. For example; $3a$, $4m$, $6p$ and $7w$ . Unlike terms can not be added or substracted.

Addition and subtraction of operations may be combined to simplify a long algebraic expression.

### Profit and loss

Profit is an excess amount of money obtained after selling goods. Profit is obtained if the selling price is greater than the buying price. Thus, to obtain the profit the following formula is used:

$$\text{Profit} = \text{Selling price} - \text{Buying price}$$

Loss is the opposite of profit. Loss is obtained when the selling price is less than the buying price. Therefore, loss is the difference between the buying and selling price. That is;

$$\text{Loss} = \text{Buying price} - \text{Selling price}$$

On 3^rd December, 2025, Aneth was given shs 23,000 by her mother to buy school requirements as follows:

2 pairs of socks @ shs 1200
10 small exercise books @ shs 600
15 pens @ shs 250
5 pencils @ shs 100 and
4 erasers @ shs 150. Prepare an invoice of quantities and find her balance after expenditures.

**Solution**

Aneth's invoice on 3^rd December, 2025 was as follows:

| Items | Cost (shs) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 2 pairs of socks @ shs 1,200 | 2,400 |
| 10 small exercise books @ shs 600 | 6,000 |
| 15 pens @ shs 250 | 3,750 |
| 5 pencils @ shs 100 | 500 |
| 4 erasers @ shs 150 | 600 |
| **Total** | **13,250** |

Aneth spent a total of 13,250 shillings. The amount left after expenditure is: shs 23000 – shs 13250 = shs 9,750

Therefore, her balance was 9,750 shillings.

### Example 2

After receiving a salary of 134,000 shillings on 26<sup>th</sup> April, 2025, Tumaini bought the following items:

Tumaini deposited the remaining amount of money in his bank account. Prepare Tumaini’s invoice and then find the amount deposited in the bank account.

**Solution**

Tumaini’s invoice on 26<sup>th</sup> April, 2025 was as follows:

| Items | Cost (shs) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 6 kg of sugar @ shs 2,400 | 14,400 |
| 3 litres of cooking oil @ shs 1,200 | 3,600 |
| 10 kg of maize flour @ shs 900 | 9,000 |
| 7 kg of rice @ shs 1,600 | 11,200 |
| **Total** | **38,200** |

Tumaini spent a total of 38,200 shillings
Amount received = shs 134,000
Expenditures = shs 38,200
The amount deposited in the bank
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;= amount received – expenditures
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;= shs 134000 – shs 38200
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;= shs 95,800

Therefore, the amount of money deposited in the bank account was shs 95,800.

2. Profit is the difference between the selling price and the buying price.

4. An invoice is a list of items sold or bought and their prices.
5. The symbol @ is used when writing invoices to represent the words “for each”.
